## 1. How a rider or driver raises a complaint or fare dispute with Ryde: channels, required information, deadlines, response times

### Takeaway
Ryde routes almost everything through the in-app "RydeHELP" ticket flow tied to a specific past trip, with support@rydesharing.com, a web ticket form and live chat as fallbacks; no telephone hotline is published. The only firm published limits are: 30 days for a rider to contest a cancellation or waiting fee, a "within 24 hours" response endeavour, a 3-day target for driver account appeals, and tight 24/48-hour windows for driver cleaning claims.

### Inferences
- A realistic mock SLA set drawn only from Ryde's own text: first response target 24 hours; rider fee-dispute filing window 30 days from trip; driver cleaning claim 48 hours from trip (cleaning within 24 hours); claim-outcome appeal 24 hours; driver suspension appeal resolved within 3 days.
- Ryde's published "No-Show" evidence test is two-part and machine-checkable (GPS at pick-up point AND more than 3 minutes waited after "I'm Here"), which maps directly onto an automated No-Show Charge decision.
- For "Route Deviation", Ryde's position is that the fare is fixed upfront so the route does not change the price (tolls aside). A route-deviation fare dispute on Ryde would therefore mostly be about tolls/ERP, added stops/destination changes, or safety, not metered distance.
- In-app chat between rider and driver is described as erased after 24 hours, so chat evidence for a dispute may not be available to the parties after that; whether Ryde retains a server copy is not stated.

### Gaps
- No published resolution-time commitment for rider complaints (only the 24-hour response endeavour). No published escalation tier (e.g. supervisor review) for riders.
- No deadline published for general fare disputes other than cancellation/waiting fee waivers, nor for a rider contesting a cleaning fee.
- No telephone hotline found; whether one exists inside the app is unknown.
- Fee amounts conflict across articles ($4.50 vs $6.61 cancellation; $4.50 vs $5 vs "$6.61" waiting). The Feb 2026 rider-facing figures ($6.61 cancellation, $5 waiting) appear current; the driver payout amount after 17 Feb 2026 is not clearly stated.

## 2. Ryde's Terms of Use: role, liability, rider-driver disputes, refund discretion, governing law, arbitration

### Takeaway
Ryde's Terms (page states "Last modified: 6th April 2022", though it contains a later-looking S$200 cleaning-fee clause) position Ryde as a technology platform that merely links users with independent "Partners", disclaim involvement in rider-driver disputes, cap liability at what the user paid for the service in question, reserve "absolute discretion" over refunds, and send disputes to SIAC arbitration under Singapore law, with a carve-out letting Partners (drivers) use the Singapore Mediation Centre and/or Small Claims Tribunal.

Where the Services are provided by a Partner (as defined below), Ryde's role is merely to link the User with such Partners. Ryde is not responsible for the acts and/or omissions of any Partner. Partners are not, and shall not hold themselves out to be, an agent, employee or staff of Ryde and the Services (as defined below) provided by Partners shall not be deemed to be provided by Ryde."

- Disputes: "Ryde will not be a party to disputes or negotiations of disputes between you and Users or Partners including merchants, advertisers and/or sponsors. Unless you are a corporate user with a current corporate account with Ryde, Ryde cannot and will not play any role in managing payments between you and the Partners".

### Inferences
- There is a gap between contract and practice: the Terms say complaints "must be taken up with each other directly" and Ryde "will not be a party to disputes", yet the help centre shows Ryde adjudicating fee waivers, cleaning claims (with "sole discretion") and suspensions, and telling drivers "Do not demand compensation directly from riders". A mock policy should model the operational practice, with the Terms as the liability backstop.
- The SMC / Small Claims Tribunal carve-out is written only for Partners (drivers). On the face of the Terms, a rider's contractual route is SIAC arbitration; whether that would bar a consumer from the Small Claims Tribunals is a legal question this research did not resolve.
- The "Last modified: 6th April 2022" date is unreliable as a version marker: the S$200 cleaning-fee sentence matches help-centre tiers edited in April and October 2026 and sits oddly in the page (a duplicate damage clause follows the "Last modified" line), suggesting later edits without a date change.

### Gaps
- No lost-property clause in the Terms. No clause on complaint deadlines or response times. No clause describing how Ryde decides rider-driver disputes or what evidence it uses.
- The Terms do not address assault/harassment reports or police referral (these appear only in the Code of Conduct).
- Not determined: whether the liability cap and arbitration clause would survive the Unfair Contract Terms Act / Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act for consumers.

## 3. How refunds are issued (RydeCoins, wallet, original payment method) and timing

### Takeaway
Ryde's Terms let it choose the refund channel (card, RydeCoins wallet, or other), and in practice the published rider remedy for a wrongly charged cancellation or waiting fee is a voucher code that tops up the Ryde wallet, not cash. No refund processing time is published for riders.

### Cited Findings
- [RYDE] Channel discretion: refunds "may be credited to the payment card you used for the journey, or to your RydeCoins wallet or by such other method as is deemed reasonable by Ryde." — [Terms of Use](https://rydesharing.com/terms-of-use/)
- [RYDE] Cancellation fee refund form: "we will issue you a voucher** with a unique code worth $6.61 as a refund for the incurred Cancellation Charges. Please be mindful to top up your Ryde wallet using the voucher code given to you within the expiry period." "**This voucher is non-refundable and cannot be exchanged for cash in part or in full. There is strictly no extension of the expiry date from the date of issue." — [Cancellation Fee Waiver Request](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/18229758389017-Cancellation-Fee-Waiver-Request)
- [RYDE] Waiting fee refund form: "a voucher** with a unique code worth $5.00 as a refund for the incurred Waiting Charges." — [Waiting Fee Waiver Request](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/27085468660505-Waiting-Fee-Waiver-Request)
- [RYDE] RydeCoins are a closed-loop stored value: "RydeCoins are solely intended for use within our Platform and are non-refundable, and cannot be redeemed, exchanged, transferred or converted into cash"; "The RydeCoins will only be valid for six (6) months from the date of the last transaction"; "RydeCoins are non-refundable and not redeemable, exchanged or transferred for cash value under any circumstances – including the closing down of one's own user account." Suspended accounts cannot use RydeCoins. — [Terms of Use, "RydeCoins – Singapore Only"](https://rydesharing.com/terms-of-use/)
- [RYDE] Wallet limits: "There is a top up limit of $1,000 per transaction"; minimum top-up "$2"; "Your RydeCoins wallet should hold a maximum of $1,000 at any one time"; "Payment has to be made in full using RydeCoins." ## 6. Singapore regulation: Point-to-Point Passenger Transport Industry Act 2019, LTA licensing, PTC fare rules

### Takeaway
Ride-hail operators with 800 or more vehicles need an LTA Ride-hail Service Operator Licence; the Act lets LTA attach conditions (including insurance and driver-payment terms) and issue codes of practice that expressly cover "customer complaints and lost property", safety incidents and accident reporting to LTA, and fare display. Fares are not price-controlled, but flat fares must be shown upfront at booking and additional charges published; fare evasion carries composition fines of $200/$400. LTA can fine a licensee up to the higher of $100,000 or 10% of annual turnover. The actual licence conditions and codes of practice are not public.

### Cited Findings

Statute (Singapore Statutes Online, "Current version as at 09 Oct 2026")
- [LAW] s 21(1): "In granting a ride‑hail service licence to any person, the LTA may impose any conditions that the LTA considers requisite or expedient having regard to the purposes of this Act." — [PPPTIA 2019, Part 4](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PPPTIA2019?ProvIds=P14-)
- [LAW] s 21(2) conditions may include: "(g) requiring the licensee to comply with applicable codes of practice"; "(h) relating to the basic terms that must be in a participating driver agreement ... including a term for payment, within a maximum period prescribed, to that authorised driver the fares or reward"; "(i) concerning insurance requirements relating to the provision of the ride‑hail service by the licensee"; "(e) requiring the provision of a performance bond, guarantee or any other form of security"; "(f) requiring the licensee to undergo (at its own cost) such audit as the LTA may require". — [PPPTIA 2019 s 21](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PPPTIA2019?ProvIds=P14-)
- [LAW] s 20(1): a ride-hail licence "is valid for the period specified in the licence and is in force unless the licence is earlier revoked or suspended under section 35." — [PPPTIA 2019 s 20](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PPPTIA2019?ProvIds=P14-)

- [LAW] s 31(1): LTA "may give a direction to a licensee for or in respect of any matter affecting the interests of the public", having regard among other things to "standards of quality, reliability and safety of those services". — [PPPTIA 2019 s 31](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PPPTIA2019?ProvIds=P15-)

You and your vehicle must also be covered with valid insurance as stipulated by local regulations." — [Ryde Code Of Conduct](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/15395890865561-Ryde-Code-Of-Conduct)

### Inferences
- The statute proves that complaint handling, lost property, safety-incident reporting to LTA and fare display are within what LTA can regulate through codes of practice, but the binding detail (time limits, reporting deadlines) sits in unpublished licence conditions and codes. ## 7. Platform Workers Act (in force 1 January 2025): what changes for drivers

### Takeaway
From 1 January 2025 ride-hail drivers are "platform workers": operators must provide work injury compensation insurance at employee-equivalent levels (medical cap $53,000 for accidents from 1 Nov 2025; death $91,000 to $269,000; permanent incapacity up to $346,000 x % incapacity), must report accidents to MOM (iReport within 10 days), and drivers can be collectively represented by registered Platform Work Associations with MOM conciliation and Industrial Arbitration Court arbitration. Injuries "while waiting for tasks" are not covered. Ryde confirms WICA cover for all its private-hire and RydeSEND drivers.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Long title: "An Act to provide for the rights and obligations of platform operators and platform workers, to register and recognise platform work associations and provide for matters connected therewith, to amend certain Acts to provide for the rights, obligations, protections and representation of platform ..." (truncated in capture). Act has Part 2 obligations including "records in respect of platform workers" (s 13) and "earnings slips for platform workers" (s 14). — [Platform Workers Act 2024 (No. 30 of 2024), current version as at 09 Oct 2026](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PWA2024)
- [LAW] "all platform operators are required to provide Work Injury Compensation (WIC) insurance to their platform workers." Coverage applies from 1 Jan 2025. — [MOM, Work injury compensation for platform workers (last updated 29 Dec 2025)](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [LAW] When covered: "When the platform worker begins to travel to the pick-up or collection location" through to completion at the drop-off. Not covered: injuries "While waiting for tasks", non-work errands between tasks, intoxication, deliberate self-harm, unprovoked fights, illegally modified or unlicensed vehicles. — [MOM WIC page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [LAW] Amounts: medical expenses up to $53,000 for accidents from 1 Nov 2025 ($45,000 before), up to one year from the accident; income loss at full average daily earnings for the first 14 days of medical leave (60 days if hospitalised) then two-thirds up to one year; permanent incapacity minimum $116,000 x % incapacity, maximum $346,000 x % incapacity; death minimum $91,000, maximum $269,000. — [MOM WIC page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [LAW] Multi-apping: liability is apportioned "through a prioritisation of work tasks"; where several operators are liable each pays a share based on earnings. — [MOM WIC page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [LAW] Reporting: fatal accidents, notify MOM as soon as possible and submit an iReport within 10 days; non-fatal, iReport within 10 days of notification. Workers can claim within one year of the accident. — [MOM WIC page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [LAW] Representation: "Since 1 January 2025, the Industrial Relations Act has been amended to provide for the regulation of the relations of platform operators and platform workers". A registered association can represent workers if it "Receives direct recognition from the platform operator" or wins a secret ballot (at least 20% of eligible workers voting, majority support). Then "it will become the sole negotiating body for the platform workers it represents". "The Collective Agreement is valid for a minimum of 2 years and a maximum of 3 years." — [MOM, Industrial relations for platform workers (last updated 9 Oct 2025)](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/industrial-relations)
- [LAW] Disputes: MOM "will proceed to arrange a conciliation meeting within 14 days"; if unresolved "the trade dispute may be referred to the IAC for arbitration." A member who believes their platform work agreement was terminated without just cause may, through the association, make representations to the Minister for Manpower within one month of termination. — [MOM, Industrial relations](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/industrial-relations)
- [3P] MOM registered the National Taxi Association, National Private Hire Vehicles Association and National Delivery Champions Association as Platform Work Associations on 7 January 2025. — [NTUC](https://www.ntuc.org.sg/uportal/news/NTA-NPHVA-NDCA-Secure-Stronger-Protection-Platform-Workers/)
- [RYDE] "You are considered a platform worker if you have an agreement with Ryde to provide ride-hail or delivery services and derive payment or benefits in Singapore. You will receive an earnings slip with your status." — [Who is a Platform Worker? (21 Jan 2025)](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/42603986150681-Who-is-a-Platform-Worker)
- [RYDE] WICA covers "All Ryde Private Hire drivers and RydeSEND drivers."; "WICA coverage will begin from 1 January 2025." — [Who does WICA Cover?](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/43870121173529-Who-does-WICA-Cover); [When does WICA coverage begin?](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/43870175029657-When-does-WICA-coverage-begin)
- [RYDE] Income-loss worked example: "[$9,000 × (1 - 60%)] ÷ 90 days = $40 per day of MC/hospitalization", with Fixed Expense Deduction Amount "Cars, vans, trucks, lorries: 60% FEDA". — [income loss article (26 Feb 2025)](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/43870228833177-How-will-my-earnings-be-calculated-for-the-income-loss-compensation-component)
- [RYDE] 20-F: "The Platform Workers Act 2024 has fully come into force on 1 January 2025."; costs include "contribution of driver partners' Central Provident Fund (CPF) and paying for driver partners' insurance"; Ryde's "[i]nsurance includes work injury compensation, director and officer liability insurance". — [Ryde 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1971115/000149315226018829/form20-f.htm)
- [3P] Ryde extended its Singlife partnership to implement a work injury compensation plan for driver-partners effective 1 January 2025, covering lump sums for permanent incapacity or death, medical leave wages and medical expenses. — [press release via Stock Titan](https://stocktitan.net/news/RYDE/ryde-extends-partnership-with-singlife-to-implement-work-injury-81sh79831k9m.html)
- [RYDE] Account-integrity rules tied to the Act: "Each account is strictly personal, non-transferable, and must only be used by the registered platform worker." Violations may bring "Suspension or permanent deactivation"; "If found guilty under the Employment of Foreign Manpower Act, you will be debarred for a minimum of 24 months". — [Driver Performance and Community Standards](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/20244107265689-Driver-Performance-and-Community-Standards); [What happens if I violate these rules? (23 Dec 2025)](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/51618350667161-What-happens-if-I-violate-these-rules)

### Inferences
- A driver injured in a Safety Incident while travelling to pick-up or carrying a passenger is now a statutory WIC case with MOM reporting duties on the operator; this is a strong reason for a rule that any driver-injury report bypasses automated resolution and goes to a human. The 10-day iReport deadline is a real, citable SLA.
- A driver assaulted by a passenger during a trip would appear to be in the course of work, but MOM's page excludes "unprovoked fights" (wording as summarised); how assault by a passenger is treated was not confirmed.
- Deactivation disputes now have a formal route for association members (representation to the Minister within one month), alongside Ryde's own 3-day appeal target.

### Gaps
- Whether any Platform Work Association has been recognised by Ryde, or any collective agreement signed, was not found.
- Section-level text of the Platform Workers Act and the amended Work Injury Compensation Act was not read (only the table of contents and long title loaded); amounts above are from MOM's page.
- Whether individual driver-operator payment disputes can go to TADM / Employment Claims Tribunals was not confirmed by any source read.

## 8. External routes for an unresolved dispute

### Takeaway
Outside Ryde, the routes are: Small Claims Tribunals (claims up to $20,000, or $30,000 by consent; within 2 years), CASE (voluntary mediation, no power to bind), LTA feedback (target reply 5 working days), PTC/LTA for fare evasion, the police for crime, lost valuables and fare evasion, and for drivers the Platform Work Association / MOM route. Ryde's Terms nominally send disputes to SIAC arbitration.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Small Claims Tribunals: "The claim limit is $20,000 (or $30,000 if there is a Memorandum of Consent" signed by both parties; covers disputes on goods, services or residential tenancies "not exceeding 2 years"; process is filing, a consultation with a registrar, then if unsettled "a hearing before a tribunal magistrate, who will decide the outcome of the claim." — [Singapore Courts, File a small claim (last updated 8 Apr 2026)](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/civil/file-small-claim)
- [RYDE] Terms route: SIAC arbitration for all users; "if you are a Partner, you may choose to submit a Dispute for mediation at the Singapore Mediation Centre and/or Small Claims Tribunal". — [Terms of Use](https://rydesharing.com/terms-of-use/)
- [LAW/3P] CASE: after an online complaint officers assess and advise; mediation is voluntary and by invitation, sessions are 2 hours, an administrative fee is charged to both parties; CASE states a resolution rate of approximately 70%. — [CASE, Submit a complaint](https://www.case.org.sg/?p=14844); [CASE mediation](https://case.org.sg/mediation)
- [LAW] CASE fee figures conflict between sources: CASE's mediation page lists (claims under $5,000) 16.20 for members and 37.80 for non-members, rising to 432 for claims above $40,000; a 2016 parliamentary reply cites a $10.70 administrative fee for an assisted letter (old). — [CASE mediation](https://case.org.sg/mediation); [MTI written reply, 2016](https://www.mti.gov.sg/Newsroom/Parliamentary-Replies/2016/11/Written-reply-to-PQ-on-consumers-pursuing-complaints-against-merchant)
- [LAW] LTA feedback: LTA says it aims to reply within 5 working days and that more complex cases may take 14 to 20 days; there is a separate reporting form for illegal ride-hailing services. — [LTA contact form](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/contact_us/contact_form.html)
- [LAW] MOT has said LTA will weigh operators' track record when assessing licence renewals. — [MOT written reply](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-holding-taxi-and-platform-operators-rather-than-drivers-accountable-for-new-operational-disruption-standards/)
- [LAW] Fare evasion: drivers report through their operator, LTA or the police; PTC and LTA enforce. — [PTC fare evasion page](https://www.ptc.gov.sg/fares/fare-evasion/taxi-private-hire-car/)
- [RYDE] Police referrals Ryde itself directs: lost valuable not found, "make a police report"; found item from unknown trip, "Make a police report immediately"; fare evasion, "file an online report with SPF"; accidents with injury, "Make a traffic accident report within 24 hours"; medical emergency "call 995". — [I lost my item](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/900003102263-I-lost-my-item); [I found an item](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011925934-I-found-an-item); [Fare Evasion](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/4842763548825-Fare-Evasion); [accident article](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/900003838386-I-was-involved-in-an-accident-What-should-I-do)
- [RYDE] Illegal platform activity: report via "go.gov.sg/report-illegal-rides", MOM's infringement e-service, "or email support@rydesharing.com". — [suspicious or illegal activity article (edited 23 Dec 2025)](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/53608088084249-What-should-I-do-if-I-see-something-suspicious-or-illegal)
- [LAW] Drivers: MOM conciliation "within 14 days" and IAC arbitration via a recognised Platform Work Association; work injury claims via MOM within one year. — [MOM industrial relations](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/industrial-relations); [MOM WIC](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/work-injury-compensation-for-platform-workers)
- [RYDE] Motor-accident injury and vehicle damage go to the driver's motor insurer, not Ryde. — [insurance article](https://help.rydesharing.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000777634-Is-there-any-insurance-coverage-in-the-event-of-an-accident)

### Inferences
- A sensible external-escalation ladder for the mock system: (1) Ryde internal review / appeal; (2) for riders, CASE mediation then Small Claims Tribunals (money claims within limits); for drivers, association / MOM, or SMC / Small Claims Tribunal as the Terms allow; (3) LTA feedback for operator or driver conduct; (4) police immediately for any alleged crime, with no need to exhaust internal steps.
- Personal-injury claims from a road accident and vehicle damage are insurer matters and fall outside a platform dispute tool's remit other than evidence preservation.

### Gaps
- The Small Claims Tribunals page read did not list which claim types are excluded (e.g. motor accident property damage, personal injury); the "Cases eligible for a small claim" page was not fetched.
- No CASE statistics or cases specific to Ryde or ride-hailing found. Current CASE fees need checking at source.
- Whether SIAC arbitration in Ryde's Terms is ever invoked against consumers, or would be enforced over the Small Claims Tribunals' jurisdiction, was not found.
- No Singapore Police Force page was fetched; police routes above are as directed by Ryde's own articles.
